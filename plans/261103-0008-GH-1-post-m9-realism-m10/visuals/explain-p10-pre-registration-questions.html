The analogy: a parcel service with two post offices

Picture a parcel service delivering to one house. There is a main post office and a smaller express depot. Every parcel goes to the main office first. A clerk there stamps it with a serial number and a label, then sends it to the house either by the main office's own truck or, through a courier road, by the express depot's fast van. Each office keeps a logbook with columns whose names come from a national rulebook. Head office reads those logbooks to decide which office should serve which house.

The rulebook is strict about what each column means. Questions P6 and P9 are about our express depot writing numbers into columns that, by the rulebook, belong to somebody else.

Where the analogy stops

A real parcel is one object. A packet can be split across both paths and retransmitted, so one stamped parcel can be loaded twice by the van (a retransmission). Also, the logbook is sent every 100 ms, not once a day, and the rulebook is two documents: 28.552 for 5G offices and 32.425 for LTE offices.

UB: the ns-3 neighbour-list bug (recommended: fix under Amendment 4)

The question. ns-3 has a bug that sometimes drops a phone's only neighbour cell from the measurement report. Should M10 fix it, or only disclose it?

In the analogy. Each depot's logbook has a line "other offices this house can also hear, strongest first". The clerk reads the list backwards and stops one step too early. When the list has exactly two offices, and the house's own office is the stronger one with the higher number, the clerk skips the only other office. The report then says "no other office in range", which is false.

In the system. The loop is at mmwave-enb-net-device.cc:1001-1003. It counts down past sortFlipMap.begin(), which is undefined behaviour (UB): C++ does not say what happens. With the GNU library we use, it drops the only neighbour whenever a UE's map has 2 cells and the serving cell has the higher cell ID and is the strongest.

Background

Why does it need a contract amendment?

The outcome contract lists exactly which simulator fixes M10 may make (the T-C list: P6, P7, P9, P15, UseIdealRrc). This bug is not on that list. Any change outside it is a scope change, which the contract says needs your approval as an amendment. That would be Amendment 4.

What does it affect?

Every two-gNB test (r0, r2, r3p) and so the stored references M9, checkpoint 1, checkpoint 2 and δ. Their recorded neighbour lists are missing entries. The 10-gNB s10 test cannot hit it, because its maps have 10 entries.

Why decide now and not at Stop B?

Checkpoint 3 (P11) records new references and tags them. Tags never move. If the fix comes after that, M10 ships with the bug in its references. So "decide at Stop B" means "never in M10".

Fix under Amendment 4 (recommended)

  • Its own ns-3 commit, right after D-R3-1 and before P6
  • A ledger row: every 2-cell tick reports exactly 1 neighbour; a 0-neighbour fixture fails
  • Cost: M4 and M5–M7, about 1 h and 3–4 GiB
  • Tests cover maps of 1, 2, 8, 9 and 10 cells and equal-strength ties, plus one 10-gNB s10 run
  • The checkpoint-3 diff then names this fix as the cause of the changed neighbour lists

Disclose only (default)

  • No code change
  • Every 2-gNB reference keeps the missing-neighbour artefact
  • Listed as a known model defect in the M10 records
  • Cannot be fixed later inside M10

Q1 / P6: a column with the wrong name (recommended: rename to ns3.*)

The question. The en-gNB reports its MAC-layer data volume under the name QosFlow.PdcpPduVolumeDL. Should that name, and every other name that fails a strict rulebook check, be renamed to a vendor name like ns3.du.macVolumeDl?

In the analogy. The express depot writes "parcels loaded into the van, counting reloads" into a column the rulebook calls "parcels the stamping desk passed to the loading bay at a depot that has its own stamping desk, not counting two-office deliveries". Our depot has no stamping desk, and every delivery is a two-office delivery. There is no right column for its number in the rulebook at all.

In the system. 3GPP TS 28.552 §5.1.3.6.1.1 (PDF pp.196–197) defines QosFlow.PdcpPduVolumeDL only for a split gNB (one with separate CU and DU parts). It explicitly excludes dual-connectivity volume. Our en-gNB is not split, and every bearer is EN-DC (dual connectivity). 28.552 has no MAC-layer volume measurement anywhere. The value is computed from MAC transport blocks, retransmissions included (mmwave-phy-trace.cc:262), and sent at metric-map.v1.json:33.

Your follow-up: why were these names used in the first place, and can they come from specs other than 28.552?

Short answer. This project did not invent most of them. 32 of the 38 non-ns3 names came with ns-O-RAN, written in 2022 by its authors (Michele Polese, Andrea Lacava, Tommaso Zugno). They appear in ns-3-mmwave-oran commit fccf3903 (2022-04-15) as CSV column headers (mmwave-enb-net-device.cc:432-458, lte-enb-net-device.cc:632-642), and in the oran-interface module that sends them over E2 (its public history starts at 8585bec, 2022-09-25, with the names already there). Our metric map inherited them. Most were copied from real specs of that time: 3GPP TS 28.552 Release 16/17 for the gNB, and 3GPP TS 32.425 for the LTE eNB. So yes, several come from other specs or older versions, and a few come from no spec at all. The project itself added only 6 names, all correct (RRU.PrbAvailDl/Ul, RRU.PrbTotDl/Ul in Testbed d487283, 2026-10-22; DRB.PdcpSduVolumeUL/DL in oran-interface 96efc40, 2026-10-27).

Why ns-O-RAN could get away with it

In 2021-22 ns-O-RAN sent its values in its own ASN.1 module (E2SM-KPM-RC, in oran-e2sim, by Lacava and Zugno), built on E2SM-KPM v1, which carried fixed container fields rather than named measurements. The 3GPP-style strings were mainly labels for the CSV logs and the dashboards, so nobody checked them against a spec. Background

What is the metric map and the lint?

ns3_e2_agent/schema/metric-map.v1.json in the Testbed fork lists every value the E2 agent reports: its KPM name, unit, type and order. The lint (lint-metric-map.py) checks that file. Contract AC16 says the lint must match each name's source, unit and type to its 28.552 clause.

Why about 20 names, not 1?

Kongming checked the whole map against 28.552. About 20 of the 38 non-vendor names fail: some do not exist in 28.552 (DRB.BufferSize.Qos, DRB.PdcpPduNbrDl.Qos, L1M.RS-SINR.Bin*), some use a different form (CARR.PDSCHMCSDist.Bin1–6), some are 5G names on the LTE node, and RRU.PrbTot* must be checked for unit. A pre-registered rule renames each failing name in place to ns3.*, so you do not need to rule name by name.

Does a rename change the data the RIC sees?

The numbers do not change. The agent sends values by measurement ID, and a rename in place keeps the ID and order, so the indication bytes stay identical. What changes is the name list in E2 Setup, the Influx field names, and kpimon's measurement list. It costs one agent rebuild, one kpimon reinstall, and M5–M7.

Q2 / P9: the depot counting the main office's stamps (recommended: Option A)

The question in one line. The en-gNB currently reports a number under a name that, by the rulebook, means something it never does. Which name should that number go under instead, and what should the old name say?

In the analogy. The depot's logbook has a column "parcels stamped at this office". The depot stamps nothing, so honestly that column is 0. Today the depot copies the main office's whole stamp count into it, including the parcels the main office delivered with its own truck. The rulebook has a different column for what the depot really handles: "parcels passed between offices over the courier road".

In the system. The name DRB.PdcpSduVolumeDL counts bits entering this node's own PDCP (3GPP TS 28.552 §5.1.2.1.1.1). The ns-3 en-gNB has no PDCP for these bearers; the eNB's McEnbPdcp numbers every packet. So by the rulebook the en-gNB's value is 0. What the en-gNB really carries is what reaches it over X2, and 28.552 has a name for exactly that: DRB.PdcpSduVolumeX2DL (§5.1.2.1.1.2). The R3′ predictor (the harness stand-in for QP) reads this counter to predict each cell's throughput.

Your follow-up questions (asked 2026-11-06 18:59)

"Which counter carries what it actually sends?" What does this mean?

"Counter" here means a measurement name in the KPM report, that is, a column in the logbook. The question was: once DRB.PdcpSduVolumeDL honestly says 0 at the en-gNB, under which name should the en-gNB report the data that really flows through it? The data that really flows through the en-gNB is the share of the UE's packets that the eNB chose to send over X2 to the en-gNB, which the en-gNB then transmits on the NR radio. 28.552 defines a measurement for exactly that: DRB.PdcpSduVolumeX2DL, "the Data Volume (amount of PDCP SDU bits) in the downlink delivered on X2 interface in DC-scenarios" (§5.1.2.1.1.2, PDF p.183–184). Option A puts the number there. A better wording of the question would have been: "Which measurement name should hold the volume the en-gNB really carries?"

Where is DRB.PdcpSduVolumeDL defined?

In 3GPP TS 28.552 V20.2.0 (Zotero 5SZUBP6M), §5.1.2.1.1.1 "DL Cell PDCP SDU Data Volume", PDF p.183. It sits in §5.1.2, "Performance measurements valid only for non-split gNB deployment scenario", and its object is NRCellCU. The definition says (c): "This measurement is obtained by counting the number of bits entering the NG-RAN PDCP layers. The measurement is performed at the PDCP SDU level." The unit is Mbit; O-RAN E2SM-KPM v03.00 §7.9.1 changes it to kbit INTEGER for the KPM report. The LTE twin is DRB.PdcpSduVolumeDl (lower-case "l") in 3GPP TS 32.425 V19.0.0 (the copy you added, Zotero 9XR9PVRW), §4.4.7.1 "DL cell PDCP SDU Data Volume": "obtained by counting the number of bits entering the eNodeB/RN", at PDCP SDU level, object EUtranCellFDD/TDD. The next clause, §5.1.2.1.1.2, defines DRB.PdcpSduVolumeX2DL. Annex A.25 (PDF p.403) explains why it exists: "in scenarios of dual connectivity, for split bearers, PDCP data is transferred between the MN and the SN … the data volume transferred between MN and SN should be counted."

Why is it 0 at the en-gNB? (step by step)

Follow one downlink packet for a UE on the EN-DC split bearer. 1. The packet arrives from the core network at the LTE eNB (the master node). 2. The eNB's PDCP (McEnbPdcp) takes it as an SDU, numbers it, adds the PDCP header, and makes it a PDU. This is the only PDCP the packet ever enters. The bits "entering the PDCP layer" are counted here, at the eNB. That is 32.425's DRB.PdcpSduVolumeDl on the eNB. 3. The eNB then picks a leg for this packet, about half to each (perPckToLTE 0.5). LTE leg: the eNB's own LTE RLC sends it (mc-enb-pdcp.cc:221). NR leg: the eNB sends the finished PDU over X2 to the en-gNB (mc-enb-pdcp.cc:238). 4. On the NR leg the en-gNB puts the PDU straight into its RLC and MAC and sends it on the NR radio. It never passes the packet through a PDCP of its own, because the en-gNB has none for this bearer. 28.552's DRB.PdcpSduVolumeDL at the en-gNB counts "bits entering the NG-RAN PDCP layers" of that node. In step 4 no bits enter a PDCP at the en-gNB, so the honest value is 0.

So what does the en-gNB report today?

Today's code (mmwave-enb-net-device.cc:672-708) adds up, for every UE the en-gNB knows, the eNB PDCP's transmit counter for that UE. That counter ticks for both legs: the same trace m_txPdu fires at mc-enb-pdcp.cc:221 (LTE leg) and :238 (X2 leg). So the en-gNB credits itself with the UE's whole PDCP volume, including the half the eNB delivered over its own LTE radio, which never touched the en-gNB. A code comment there already says so: "this is the MN-hosted volume credited to this cell". Two side effects: a UE listed by two en-gNBs during a cell change is credited to both, and the eNB's own LTE volume is counted a second time at the en-gNB.

Your follow-up (19:35): what does DRB.PdcpSduVolumeX2DL count when the MN has n SNs? Only what went to one SN, or n times?

Only what actually crossed X2 into that SN's cell. Nothing is multiplied by n. Who reports it. The measurement's object is NRCellCU (28.552 §5.1.2.1.1.2 f), so each en-gNB reports it for its own cell. Its definition (c) is "the number of bits transferred in the downlink through X2 interface", counted at PDCP SDU level. An en-gNB can only see the X2 traffic that arrives at it, so each SN's value is its own share. Why the shares add up instead of multiplying. In EN-DC "a UE is connected to one eNB that acts as a MN and one en-gNB that acts as a SN" (37.340 V19.3.0 §4.1.2, PDF p.12). One eNB can serve many UEs, each with its own SN, so the eNB may talk to n en-gNBs at once. But each packet of a UE goes over X2 once, to that UE's one SN. So if the eNB sends 1,000 packets to SN 1 and 600 to SN 2, SN 1 reports 1,000 and SN 2 reports 600. The sum over all SNs, 1,600, equals what the eNB sent over X2 in total. In ns-3 this holds too: the eNB sends each X2 packet to exactly one targetCellId (mc-enb-pdcp.cc:232-238), and Option A credits it to that cell only. The one place it can double. During an SN change, the old SN forwards its unsent packets to the new SN over X2-U (lte-enb-rrc.cc:1160-1219). Those packets cross X2 twice: MN to old SN, then old SN to new SN. Read literally, "transferred through X2" could count them at both SNs, so the network-wide sum would exceed the eNB's total by the forwarded amount (only the packets in flight at that moment, not n times). The pre-registered definition counts only MN-to-SN sends and excludes SN-to-SN forwarding, and the records disclose that choice. The per-cell volume check then compares each cell's value against exactly the packets the eNB sent to that cell.

In the analogy. The main post office follows a different national rulebook from the express depot. We only have the depot's rulebook on the shelf.

Q4 / P7: the size of the serial number on each label (recommended: 12 bits)

The question. P7 gives the EN-DC bearer a real NR PDCP header (37.340 §4.2.2 says NR PDCP is mandatory for it). The NR header carries a sequence number (SN), a counter that orders packets. 38.323 allows 12 or 18 bits. Which?

In the analogy. The stamp has room for either a 4-digit or a 6-digit serial number. A short serial number wraps around to 0 sooner; a long one makes each stamp slightly bigger.

In the system. At 12 bits the SN wraps every 4,096 packets (about every 2 s here) and the header is 2 bytes. At 18 bits it wraps every 262,144 packets (about every 131 s, which our live runs never reach) and the header is 3 bytes. Both are legal for a data bearer (38.323 Table 6.3.2-1, 38.331 PDCP-Config).

Note

A correction kongming made

The researcher said only 18 bits would make the NR header look different from the old LTE header on the wire. That is wrong: LTE also has an 18-bit format with the same bytes (36.323 §6.2.11). No SN width proves 'this is NR' from the bytes alone. Kongming first still recommended 18 bits for other reasons. Codex disagreed, and after checking the code kongming changed to 12 bits (next panel).

12 bits (recommended by both)

  • Enough for this traffic rate (one packet every 500 µs, scenario-zero.cc:691)
  • No widening of 16-bit SN fields, the shared PDCP status, or the X2 SN Status Transfer (16-bit fields, 4,096-bit bitmap)
  • Wraps about every 2 s, so the separate wrap-bug fix is exercised in every live run
  • Byte-identical to today's header, so P7 is proved by code-level checks, not by a wire difference

18 bits

  • The usual eMBB setting (kongming's belief, not verified)
  • Every packet grows by 1 byte, so P7 shows in the checkpoint-3 diff and a missed code path fails loudly
  • Never wraps in a live run, so the wrap fix is never exercised live
  • Wider SN types through ns-3, the PDCP status and X2

Warning

When 18 bits would be the right answer

Choose 18 bits if you read AC16's 'the traces show an NR PDCP header' as requiring a visible wire difference between checkpoint 2 and checkpoint 3. At 12 bits, that AC16 trace check also passes on today's code, so it would be recorded openly as a format check, not as proof of the fix. The proof then comes from three checks: (a) a lint that fails if any EN-DC code path still uses the LTE header class, with the audit line printing the class; (b) P7's runs must match the wrap-fix commit's runs byte for byte; (c) the wrap test fails on today's code and passes after the fix.

Background: the existing wrap bug

What is the SN-wrap bug?

ns-3 keeps some packet buffers in a map keyed by the raw SN (lte-rlc-am.h:208, filled at lte-rlc-am.cc:1808), so after a wrap it sorts 0, 1 before 4094, 4095. Two handover checks compare SN >= SN_1 - 2 (lte-enb-rrc.cc:1105, lte-ue-rrc.cc:1774), which goes wrong when SN_1 is 0 or 1. Packets just before the wrap are silently left out. It does not crash. Both advisors recommend fixing it in its own commit right before P7: at 12 bits it fires live, and a separate commit lets P11 tell its effect apart from P7's.

Your follow-up: the proposed wrap fix, step by step

Where it matters. Both bugs sit in the code that runs when a UE changes cell. At that moment the old cell hands its unsent and unacknowledged packets to the new cell ("forwarding"), so no data is lost. The SN wrap matters only if the packets being handed over straddle the wrap, for example SNs 4094, 4095, 0, 1. Today's header is already 12-bit, so this can happen in today's runs too, about once every 2 s of traffic per UE. Part 1: keep the forwarded packets in sending order. CreateRlcSduBuffer() (lte-rlc-am.cc:1798-1810) copies the packets into a std::map keyed by the raw SN, and the forwarding code walks that map from smallest key to largest (lte-enb-rrc.cc:1058-1069). For 4094, 4095, 0, 1 the map gives 0, 1, 4094, 4095, so the new cell receives the newest packets first. The fix orders by distance from the oldest packet instead: key = (SN − oldest SN) mod 4,096. Then 4094 → 0, 4095 → 1, 0 → 2, 1 → 3, and the order is right. Keeping the list in the order the RLC already holds it is an equivalent fix. Part 2: find the right "previous" packets. The code also adds the up to 2 packets just before the first forwarded one (SN_1). It tests SN >= SN_1 - 2 && SN <= SN_1 (lte-enb-rrc.cc:1105; the UE side has the same test at lte-ue-rrc.cc:1774). SN_1 − 2 is computed as a signed number, so when SN_1 is 1 the lower bound is −1, and 4095 (the packet just before 0) is never selected. When SN_1 is 0, the bound is −2, and 4094 and 4095 are both missed. The fix uses modular distance: select SN if (SN_1 − SN) mod 4,096 is 0, 1 or 2. For SN_1 = 1 this picks 4095, 0 and 1, as intended. How it is proved. A new ns-3 unit test (check c) builds exactly these cases: a buffer straddling the wrap, and SN_1 equal to 0 and 1. It must fail on today's code (proving the test can see the bug) and pass after the fix. Live runs also exercise the fixed code, because at 12 bits the SN wraps every ~2 s. The fix gets its own commit and ledger row, between P6 and P7.

Your follow-up: why 12 bits rather than 18, in more detail

1. The spec does not decide it. 38.323 Table 6.3.2-1 and 38.331 PDCP-Config allow 12 or 18 bits for a data bearer; the operator chooses. So the choice is about what works best here. 2. 12 bits is enough here. The reordering window is half the SN space: 2,048 packets at 12 bits. At one packet every 500 µs that is about 1 s of traffic, while the X2 delay is 1 ms and RLC retransmission recovery is far shorter than 1 s. Nothing in these runs comes close to needing 1 s of packets in flight. 3. 18 bits would ripple through shared LTE code. Today the SN is stored as a 16-bit number everywhere: in the master and UE PDCP (mc-enb-pdcp.h:226-235, mc-ue-pdcp.h:195-201), in the PDCP status shared with plain LTE (lte-pdcp.h:103-104), and in the X2 "SN Status Transfer" message used at handover, which carries 16-bit SNs and a 4,096-bit receive bitmap (epc-x2-header.cc:1810-1823). An 18-bit SN does not fit in 16 bits, so every one of these must widen; a missed one silently cuts the SN. At 12 bits none of them changes, and P7 becomes a header-class swap. 4. The bytes cannot prove "NR" at either width. The NR 12-bit header has the same bytes as the LTE 12-bit header, and the NR 18-bit header has the same bytes as the LTE 18-bit header (36.323 §6.2.11). So 18 bits would show that something changed, not that it is NR. The NR proof must come from code-level checks either way. 5. 12 bits keeps the wrap fix honest. At 18 bits the SN wraps only once per ~131 s of traffic, which kongming found our live runs never reach, so the fixed code would never run live. At 12 bits it runs every ~2 s. The case for 18 bits. It is the usual setting for EN-DC data bearers in real networks (kongming's belief, not checked against a source). Every packet grows by one byte, so P7 is visible in the checkpoint-3 diff, and any code path that still writes the old header would fail loudly instead of silently. Choose it if you want P7 to show on the wire.

A trap Codex found: the packet tag that disappears

The draft planned to mark each packet with a tag saying "NR header" and to stop with a fatal error if the tag was missing. But during a handover the eNB strips all tags (RemoveAllPacketTags(), lte-enb-rrc.cc:1190) before it forwards the packet (:1219). The draft's design would therefore crash on a repeated SN change. The fix: take the header type from the bearer itself, not from a tag.

L2: sub-counters written into the name (recommended: the lint flags them; no label support in P6)

What is a sub-counter, and what is a Measurement Label?

Many 3GPP measurements can be split into parts. 28.552 writes the part into the name: DRB.UEThpDl.QOS means "UE throughput, one counter per QoS class", and TB.TotNbrDl.X means "TB count, one counter per MIMO layer X". Each part is a sub-counter. KPM sends names over E2 differently. Its Measurement Name must be the plain 3GPP name, and the part goes in a separate field next to it, the Measurement Label (KPM v03.00 §8.3.9, p.30: "The subcounters are represented by the Measurement Labels defined in 8.3.11"). §8.3.11 (pp.30–32) lists the labels KPM has: PLMN, slice, 5QI, QFI, QCI, MU-MIMO layer, distribution bins X/Y/Z, a few _Filter bounds, and "noLabel", meaning "without any subcounter". There is no label for modulation (QPSK, 16QAM, 64QAM). So TB.TotNbrDl.1 in our map is a KPM mistake in form: KPM wants name TB.TotNbrDl with label "MU-MIMO layer = 1", or name TB.TotNbrDl with noLabel if the value is the total over all layers.

Which names in our map have this problem?

TB.TotNbrDl.1 and TB.ErrTotalNbrDl.1 (".1" = layer 1), DRB.EstabSucc.5QI and DRB.RelActNbr.5QI (the literal text ".5QI", not even a real 5QI number), DRB.BufferSize.Qos and DRB.PdcpPduNbrDl.Qos, the bins CARR.PDSCHMCSDist.Bin1–6 and L1M.RS-SINR.Bin*, and the modulation names TB.TotNbrDlInitial.Qpsk/16Qam/64Qam.

Why not just add real label support?

Neither side can carry labels today. The agent sends noLabel for every row (kpm-indication-message.cpp:48-53, :144-150, :196-198) and ignores labels a subscription asks for (kpm-subscription-admission.cpp:115-132). kpimon subscribes with noLabel and stores values by name in Influx, so two rows with the same name and different labels would overwrite each other. Real labels would need a map-schema change, about 50 lines in the agent encoder, a kpimon Go/C patch, a second Influx rename and a new golden file: its own commit with one more rebuild, kpimon reinstall and M5–M7 (about 1 h and 3–5 GiB). And after the value checks, no row that keeps a 3GPP name needs a label.

What the recommended rule does, row by row

The lint parses each 3GPP name and fails a suffix whenever KPM has a label for that kind of sub-counter. The row may then drop the suffix and use the bare name with noLabel only if (a) the clause defines a total, and (b) the value really is that total. Otherwise the row becomes ns3.*. Modulation suffixes stay in the name, because KPM has no label for them and 28.552 lists those exact names. Vendor ns3.* names may not reuse a 3GPP name (so today's ns3.du.QosFlow.PdcpPduVolumeDL is renamed too). Result: one row becomes a cleaner 3GPP name. gNB cell TB.TotNbrDl.1 becomes TB.TotNbrDl: the value counts every TB (mmwave-phy-trace.cc:248) and the scenario has one antenna (scenario-zero.cc:525-527), so the total over layers equals layer 1. Every other suffixed row fails for a deeper reason anyway (no total form exists in 28.552, a constant 0, an invented base name, bins with the wrong form, or modulation counts that include retransmissions) and becomes ns3.*.

L4: may a cell measurement also be sent per UE? (recommended: yes, register the KPM §7.9 check)

What is the question?

Our agent sends two lists: a cell list (one value per cell) and a UE list (one value per UE). 3GPP defines almost every measurement per cell. KPM v03.00 §7.9 (pp.13–14) says how a 3GPP cell measurement may be turned into a per-UE one: "the formulas specified in 3GPP are used with restriction to the individual UE". But it does this only for the types in its table: throughput, delay, data volume, in-session activity time, PDCP drop rate, IP latency, radio resource utilization, RRC connections / PDU sessions / DRBs / QoS flows, mobility management, CQI and MCS, and released calls. Transport-block (TB) counts are not in the table.

Which rows does it affect?

Only the UE-list rows that would otherwise keep a 3GPP name: gNB UE TB.TotNbrDl.1 (would become TB.TotNbrDl) and gNB UE TB.TotNbrDlInitial. With the §7.9 check registered, both become ns3.* names (for example ns3.du.tbTotNbrDl), because KPM gives no rule for a per-UE TB count. DRB.UEThpDl on the UE list is fine: throughput is the first row of the table. The other UE rows are already ns3.* for other reasons.

Why recommend the check?

It is the same principle as the rest of P6: keep a 3GPP name only where a spec says exactly what it means. Without the check, the per-UE TB count would carry a 3GPP name that no spec defines per UE. It costs nothing extra: the two renames ride in the same map change. Kongming's confidence is medium, because §7.9 is a table of types rather than an explicit "nothing else is allowed" rule. The alternative reading is that "restrict the 3GPP formula to one UE" works for any count, so per-UE TB counts could keep TB.TotNbrDl and TB.TotNbrDlInitial.

L5: data type of a kept 3GPP name (recommended: must match the 3GPP type, with KPM §7.9.1's overrides)

What is the question?

Every 3GPP measurement says in item (d) whether its value is an integer or a real number. KPM sends either an integer or a REAL value, and §7.9.1 (p.14) changes a few (the PDCP volumes become INTEGER in kbit instead of Mbit). That rule protects kpimon from overflow but ignores the spec. The lint therefore needs a stated rule for kept 3GPP names.

I checked every row that will keep a 3GPP name against 28.552 V20.2.0.
