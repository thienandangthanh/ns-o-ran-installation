ns-O-RAN → O-RAN SC L-release port · NIST Testbed 1.8.0

Two clocks: ns-3 sim time and RIC wall time

ns-3 advances simulated time event by event, about 32× slower than real time in the default scenario. The Near-RT RIC runs on the real clock. The port keeps free run as the default and adds an opt-in lock-step mode in which ns-3 is the only time base and stops after every indication period, so that RIC controls land at a known simulated step.

Your four questions, answered briefly

1 · Tweaks that affect xApps in a real network?

Yes, somebut not the timing machinery

The pacer, barrier, pinned epoch and ns3.* correlation KPIs live in ns-3, the agent and the harness. They don't change any xApp. What would break against real gNBs is the encoding on the TS xApp → rc path: P1 (control sent to the target cell's node), P11 (9-octet target instead of an NR CGI), P4 (IMSI in AMF-UE-NGAP-ID), N5/P2 (TS xApp cell map built from 5 raw bytes in e2nodeComponentRequestPart), P8 (RC not announced in E2 Setup). The xApp patches themselves are harmless or spec-correcting: kpimon TimeStamp SIZE(8), substitution 2 (a do-nothing RMR handler) and substitution 3 (tolerant A1 payload parsing). The stub QP, R3′ predictor and scripted 30003 trigger are harness stand-ins and have no real-network role.

2 · Is the timing approach O-RAN compliant?

Wire: yes · semantics: idealised · one deviation (P12)

The timing fields on the wire follow the specs: colletStartTime is an 8-octet NTP-64 TimeStamp at the start of the collection window, and every RIC control gets exactly one ack or failure. The specs ask the interface to support loops of 10 ms to 1 s. They set no minimum delay, so d = 0 (P3) is idealised rather than forbidden. Our wall-clock RIC half takes 5–13 ms, which is inside that range. The real gap: kpimon subscribes with a 10 s reporting period, and the agent ignores it and reports every 100 ms. This is now recorded as deviation P12 (2026-10-29); its fix needs a brainstorm and a plan first. Pausing the whole RAN has no counterpart in the specs; the closest is INSERT with a RIC Time to Wait, which pauses one procedure, not the RAN. Details and clauses.

3 · Can the xApp act in time, at the significant moment?

In lock-step: trivially · in reality: untested

At a scheduled trigger step in lock-step, the simulation freezes while the RIC works, so any loop shorter than Tctl = 30 s costs 0 simulated time. The measured wall latency L was 5–13 ms. That says nothing about speed, because we removed time from the loop. A control the xApp sends on its own almost always arrives while ns-3 is computing (about 0.7 s per period), so it is refused as late. And the TS xApp doesn't choose the moment: the harness injects the 30003 anomaly message at pre-registered steps, standing in for AD. In free run the RIC looks about 7× faster than it is (32× in the default scenario). In a real network with stock kpimon, data would arrive every 10 s. Try the latency translator.

4 · Does ns-3 pause every interval T?

Lock-step: yes · free run: never

With --e2Pacing=lockstep, ns-3 stops after every sample period, at sim time k·T + 0.8 ms (T = 100 ms). It waits until every agent has sent step k and the pacer has sent go k. step k means the agent has read sample k and called SCTP send; an agent sends it even if it dropped the sample, and a disconnected agent counts as done. The barrier does not wait for e2term, kpimon or InfluxDB; only R3′ trigger steps wait for InfluxDB, in prepare. Measured in M9: a median pause of 0.5–0.8 ms, and trigger steps of 0.22 s (prepare) up to 1.2 s (prepare plus a 1 s absence window). In total ns-3 was paused 2.7 s of a 74 s R3′ run.

Step-by-step: what the port does with time

  1. ns-3 is the only clock. Every sample carries ns3.simTimeMs and ns3.seq. Gates join InfluxDB to the ns-3 trace on (nodeId, seq[, imsi]), never on _time, because kpimon stamps time.Now().
  2. One epoch maps sim time to an absolute time. hello.simStartEpochMs plus sim time gives the KPM colletStartTime (start of the collection window, NTP-64). Gated runs pin it to 1767225600000 (scripts/gates/rung-config.json:48), so header bytes repeat across runs.
  3. Start barrier at sim time 1 µs: every agent must report READY with a subscription (Tstart 120 s).
  4. Barrier after every sample k. ns-3 flushes IPC, sends ready k to the pacer, then polls until every node's step ≥ k and the pacer's go ≥ k. GateTimeout (10 s) counts only while blocked.
  5. Trigger steps (pre-registered): after steps k the pacer runs prepare (N6 certifies Influx rows for [k−15, k], the predictor answers, the stub is loaded), then the trigger (30003 to the TS xApp, or an rc gRPC call), then waits for controlRx within Tctl, or for a W = 1 s absence window when no control is expected.
  6. Apply at the barrier. Buffered controls with afterSeq = k are applied at sim time k·T in the order (IMSI, via node, target, ctlId), each with one ledger line and one controlAck. A control stamped with an earlier seq is refused as late.

Warnings and failure modes

Lock-step hides latency. A green lock-step gate proves the loop is correct and repeatable, not that it is fast enough. An xApp needing 3 s would still pass under Tctl = 30 s.
d = 0 (P3). A control acts at the same simulated instant as the sample it reacted to. That is optimistic, even compared with the 10 ms lower bound of the Near-RT band.
The barrier doesn't wait for the RIC's database. step k means "the agent has read and sent sample k". Only trigger steps wait for InfluxDB (the N6 certification in prepare).
Unscheduled controls are refused in lock-step. ns-3 spends about 0.7 s of wall time computing each period. A control the xApp sends on its own carries afterSeq k but reaches barrier k+1, so it is refused as late. Lock-step tests whether a decision is right at a moment the harness chose. It does not test whether the xApp is fast enough.
"Applied" means the handover started. The ledger's applied is the handover's start, not completed radio execution. L is measured from the trigger to controlRx and leaves out the R3′ prepare step (≈0.22 s).
The xApp doesn't pick the moment. 30003 triggers are pre-registered from a dry run. Detecting the significant moment (AD) is out of scope, so "acting at the right moment" is untested.
Free run isn't repeatable. A control lands at a step that depends on wall-clock timing, as in the original ns-O-RAN.
Timestamps are synthetic. With a pinned epoch, every gated run claims to have happened on 2026-01-01. InfluxDB _time is wall time, so don't correlate on it.
Free-run output is unchanged by the port. Without controls, the hub writes nothing and schedules nothing (null-control equivalence gate).

Advisor reads (kongming · Codex)

Where they agree
  • The timing machinery sits below the xApps; no xApp has a simulator-clock dependence.
  • The real-network blockers are P1, P4, P11, P2/N5 and P8, not timing.
  • Wire timing (NTP-64 colletStartTime, one answer per control) is compliant. Timing semantics are idealised (d = 0, a frozen RAN).
  • Ignoring the subscribed reporting period is a real spec gap.
  • Lock-step proves repeatability, not reaction speed. Free run's landing step depends on wall-clock timing.
  • The original online path had the same sim/wall split. The gym's semaphores were the only original pause, and they ran without a RIC. Our lock-step brings the gym's model onto the live E2 wire.
What each adds
  • kongming: unscheduled controls are refused as late in lock-step; kpimon subscribes at 10 s (now P12); rc may panic on an ack without an outcome; QP's wall-clock window; the M9 pause table; avoid RealtimeSimulatorImpl, since even 1 gNB runs 1.7× slower than real time.
  • Codex: the original KPM timestamp was raw Unix ms, not NTP-64; step k is sent even for a dropped sample; "applied" means the handover started; L leaves out prepare; keep the full observation-to-actuation timestamps.
Merged recommendation for the realism pass (ranked)
  1. Sim-time actuation delay δ (ActuationDelay, default 0, byte-equal to the M9 references). Sweep {100, 200, 500 ms} plus one off-grid value before publishing any closed-loop number. Both advisors rank this first.
  2. P12 (recorded): honour the subscription with a configurable kpimon period plus agent option B. Brainstorm and plan first.
  3. Model the whole loop latency in sim time: fixed first, then seeded distributions from measurements, keeping the measurement, reception, decision, apply and completion times.
  4. Put wall-window xApps (QP, AD) on measurement time before they join the loop.
  5. Decide how lock-step treats unscheduled controls: a window W every step, or late-apply labelled statistical, or event-driven pausing later.
  6. Run free run as a measurement campaign (L, rxSeq − afterSeq, C/T, an optimized build), not as a gate.
  7. Real-time simulator only after compute headroom exists. Model execution latency with UseIdealRrc=false.

Glossary and analogy limits

sim time
ns-3's virtual clock (Simulator::Now()); it jumps from event to event and runs as fast as the CPU allows.
wall time
The real clock that the RIC, kpimon, InfluxDB and every timeout use.
T (period)
indicationPeriodicity, 100 ms of sim time between samples.
barrier
The point after sample k where ns-3 blocks in lock-step.
step k / go k
Agent: "read sample k". Pacer: "you may pass barrier k".
afterSeq
The sample a control reacted to; it must be applied at that barrier.
L
Wall latency from a trigger to the control reaching ns-3 (controlRx).
Tctl, W
30 s control deadline; 1 s absence window for "no control" decisions.
slowdown
Wall seconds per sim second: 1.685 (1 gNB), 31.96 (default).
Analogy
Lock-step is like a chess clock that stops while the RIC "thinks". That breaks down because in a real RAN the clock never stops.
